# Matched comparative docking: replacement text

## Methods addition

To evaluate the structural rationale for using triptolide in downstream studies after 16-hydroxytriptolide had been prioritized computationally, we performed a matched comparative docking analysis. The PubChem-defined structures of triptolide (CID 107985) and 16-hydroxytriptolide (CID 126556) were prepared using the same conformer-generation and ligand-preparation workflow. Twenty stereochemically defined conformers were generated using ETKDGv3 with fixed random seeds, minimized using MMFF94s, and the lowest-energy conformer of each compound was converted to PDBQT format using Meeko 0.7.1 with Gasteiger charges. Structural similarity was quantified using chiral Morgan and MACCS fingerprints and maximum-common-substructure coverage.

Both compounds were docked to the same calcium- and zinc-bound human S100A8 homodimer structures derived from PDB 5HLO. The A/C and B/D crystallographic dimers were analysed independently. Each compound underwent matched surface-wide docking using the same overlapping 28 × 28 × 28 Å boxes and exhaustiveness of 32. The shared candidate interface region was subsequently evaluated using the same 24 × 24 × 24 Å local boxes, Vina and Vinardo scoring functions, exhaustiveness of 64, and ten predefined random seeds per receptor and scoring function. Cross-compound pose similarity was assessed as the direct RMSD of the 26-heavy-atom common scaffold in the common receptor coordinate frame. Residue contacts were defined using a 4.5 Å heavy-atom cutoff. This comparison was used only to assess predicted binding-site compatibility and was not considered experimental validation of 16-hydroxytriptolide.

## Results replacement

Triptolide and 16-hydroxytriptolide showed high structural similarity (chiral Morgan Tanimoto, 0.831; MACCS Tanimoto, 0.885), and their maximum common substructure covered all 26 heavy atoms of triptolide and 26 of the 27 heavy atoms of 16-hydroxytriptolide. In surface-wide docking, both compounds independently prioritized the same S100A8 homodimer-interface region. For the B/D dimer, the same grid was ranked first for both compounds. For the A/C dimer, the same two overlapping interface grids were ranked first and second, although their order was reversed.

Across ten predefined random seeds, 16-hydroxytriptolide showed reproducible local docking poses in both crystallographic dimers and with both scoring functions (median within-condition heavy-atom RMSD, 0.025–0.048 Å; maximum, 0.104 Å). The representative triptolide and 16-hydroxytriptolide poses showed common-scaffold RMSDs of 0.13–0.62 Å and contact-residue Jaccard indices of 0.90–1.00. In the B/D dimer, both compounds contacted the same set of interface residues, including Pro43, Gln44, Tyr45, Ala82, Lys85, Ser86 and Glu89 from one protomer and Leu2, Glu6, Asn10 and Ile13 from the opposing protomer. These matched calculations support the structural plausibility of using triptolide as a related compound for exploratory downstream studies of the predicted S100A8 interface. They do not establish functional equivalence, shared experimental affinity or experimental validation of 16-hydroxytriptolide. Accordingly, all molecular-dynamics and cell-based conclusions in this study are restricted to triptolide.

## Response-to-reviewer wording

We agree that structural similarity alone was insufficient to justify the transition from the computationally prioritized 16-hydroxytriptolide to triptolide, which was used in the downstream molecular-dynamics and experimental studies. To address this concern, we performed a matched comparative docking analysis using identical S100A8 receptor structures, surface-search grids, local docking boxes, scoring functions and predefined random seeds. Both compounds independently localized to the same predicted S100A8 homodimer-interface region. Their representative poses showed common-scaffold RMSDs of 0.13–0.62 Å and 90–100% overlap of contact-residue sets. We have added these results as evidence of predicted binding-site compatibility.

We have also revised the manuscript to maintain a strict compound-specific distinction. The comparison does not establish functional equivalence or experimental binding of 16-hydroxytriptolide. All molecular-dynamics, CETSA and cell-based findings apply exclusively to triptolide, whereas 16-hydroxytriptolide remains the computationally prioritized candidate from the repositioning analysis.
